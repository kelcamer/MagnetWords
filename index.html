← Special Interests Fisher-Price magnets · 1971–1990

Type anything. Every letter takes its magnet colour, and the colour belongs to the letter, so every E is the same blue wherever it lands. Each word turns into a strip you can line up against the others.

Type

Accented letters take their base letter's colour. Digits and punctuation had no magnets, so they stay grey. The text is saved in the link, so copying the address shares it.

      six colours, cycling from A

      The colours are the Fisher-Price plastic alphabet magnets. Witthoft, Winawer & Eagleman (2015) found more than 6% of 6,588 American grapheme-colour synesthetes learned many of their letter colours from this toy, and about 15% of those born 1975–1980. PLoS ONE 10(3):e0118996 · PMID 25739095
